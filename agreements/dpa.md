# How this agreement is built

> lead: A cover page with the details of the processing. Common Paper's standard terms carry the duties. Greypillar's changes make some of them stricter.

## 1 What this agreement is {#what status=planned}

> In plain words: When you put personal data into Greypillar, Greypillar handles it only for you, as you instruct, and this agreement says how. Greypillar handles no customer's personal data, in any country, until its insurance is in place (section 13). The notes in the margin help you read it. If a note and a clause differ, the clause governs.

1.1 This DPA has two parts: (1) the Key Terms on this Cover Page, including the Greypillar changes and the Annexes, and (2) the Common Paper DPA Standard Terms Version 1.1 posted at https://commonpaper.com/standards/data-processing-agreement/1.1/ ("DPA Standard Terms"), which are incorporated by reference.[^1] If there is any inconsistency between the parts of the DPA, the Cover Page controls over the DPA Standard Terms. Capitalized words have the meanings given on the Cover Page. If the Cover Page omits or does not define a highlighted word, its meaning is "none" or "not applicable" and the clause that uses it does not apply. All other capitalized words have the meanings given in the DPA Standard Terms or the Agreement.

1.2 The Greypillar changes in sections 3 to 14 are part of the Cover Page. Each names the DPA Standard Terms section it changes.

1.3 The notes headed "In plain words" are reading aids, not part of the DPA. English governs; translations carry the same meaning.

# Greypillar changes

> lead: Where Greypillar is stricter than the standard terms, the change says so. Where the standard terms assume something Greypillar does not have, such as an audit report, the change says what replaces it.

## 3 Roles {#roles status=planned}

> In plain words: For your data, you decide and Greypillar acts for you. For its own account records, such as who signed and who pays, Greypillar decides, and its privacy notice covers them.

3.1 This section adds to DPA Standard Terms Section 1.

3.2 For Customer Personal Data, Greypillar is a Processor, or a Subprocessor where Customer is itself a Processor. For Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 ("PDPA"), Greypillar is a data processor and will meet the duties of PDPA section 40.[^4] For the GDPR, this DPA is the contract Article 28(3) requires.[^5]

3.3 Greypillar is a Controller only of the account, billing, contact and security records it keeps to run its relationship with Customer, and the privacy notice at /legal/privacy covers them. Those records are not Customer Personal Data.

3.4 Where Customer connects its own AI provider account to the Service, that provider processes under Customer's own agreement with it. It is Customer's processor, not Greypillar's Subprocessor, and Customer is responsible for that agreement. Customer may use its own AI provider account, and Greypillar adds no markup to it.

## 4 Instructions and changes to the processing {#instructions status=planned}

> In plain words: Greypillar processes your personal data only as you instruct. It cannot widen what it does with your data by sending a notice. A wider use needs 30 days' warning, and you can leave if you object.

4.1 This section changes DPA Standard Terms Section 2.3.

4.2 Greypillar may change the details in Annex I(B) by notice only where the change does not add a purpose, a Category of Data Subjects, a Category of Personal Data or Special Category Data, and does not lengthen the Duration of Processing. Any other change takes effect no earlier than 30 days after notice. If Customer objects within that period, Greypillar will not apply the change to Customer Personal Data, or, if it cannot provide the Service without it, Customer may end the affected Order Forms without charge, and Greypillar refunds prepaid Fees for the period after the end, pro rata, and unused Paid Credits.

4.3 Where Greypillar believes an instruction breaks Applicable Data Protection Laws, it tells Customer at once and need not follow that instruction until Customer confirms or changes it.

## 5 No training, no sale {#training status=planned}

> In plain words: Your personal data never trains a model and is never sold. A contribution, if you ever make one, needs its own signed grant, and it may not hold personal data unless that data is made anonymous first.

5.1 Greypillar does not train models on Customer Personal Data. Greypillar will not use Customer Personal Data to train or improve any model, and will not sell it or use it for advertising.

5.2 A contribution of Customer data happens only under a Contribution Grant made under the AI Addendum. A Contribution Grant may not include Customer Personal Data unless that data is anonymised before any use for Training, so that no individual can be identified from it.

## 6 Subprocessors {#subprocessors status=planned decides}

> In plain words: Every company that handles your personal data for Greypillar is listed publicly, or named in your order for work we do for you. A new one is announced at least 30 days before Greypillar uses it. If you object and we cannot resolve it, you can leave without charge and get back what you prepaid.

6.1 This section changes DPA Standard Terms Section 2.6(a).

6.2 The Approved Subprocessors are (a) those on the list at /trust/subprocessors on the date the DPA starts, and (b) for customer work, those named in the row "Subprocessors for this work" of the Order Form or pilot Order Form, for the tasks and in the countries stated there. Customer approves them by entering into the DPA and by signing that Order Form. Any later addition or replacement follows sections 6.3 and 6.4.

6.3 Greypillar will publish any intended addition or replacement of a Subprocessor on the list at /trust/subprocessors, and notify Customer by email to its Notice Address, at least 30 days before the new Subprocessor first Processes Customer Personal Data. The notice gives the Subprocessor's identity, country of location and anticipated Processing task. Customers may also follow changes through the feed at greypillar.ai/trust.

6.4 Customer may object within the 30 days, on reasonable grounds related to data protection. If it does, the parties will work in good faith to resolve the objection. If they have not resolved it before the change would take effect, Greypillar will not use the new Subprocessor for Customer Personal Data, or Customer may end the affected Order Forms by notice without charge, and Greypillar refunds prepaid Fees for the period after the end, pro rata, and unused Paid Credits.

6.5 Greypillar may stop using a Subprocessor at any time. Removal needs no notice in advance, but Greypillar updates the list.

6.6 In Clause 9(a) of the EEA SCCs, the time period for prior notice of Subprocessor changes is 30 days.

## 7 Who can access the data, and from where {#access status=planned}

> In plain words: Only people and agents who need it can reach your data. Section 7.2 says from where. Reaching data from abroad is a transfer, and section 12 covers it.

7.1 Greypillar limits access to Customer Personal Data to its personnel and agents who need it to provide the Service, and binds each person to confidentiality.

7.2 Greypillar personnel access Customer Personal Data only from Thailand. Greypillar announces any new place from which it would access Customer Personal Data at least 30 days before it is used, in the same way as a new Subprocessor under section 6.3, and section 6.4 applies to it. Agents act only within limits set by authorised personnel, and agents that read a copy of Customer Content cannot change or send it unless the Order Form allows it.

## 8 Security incidents {#incidents status=planned}

> In plain words: If your personal data is breached, we tell you within 48 hours of knowing, so you can meet your own deadlines, and keep you informed while we fix it.

8.1 This section changes DPA Standard Terms Section 4.1(a).

8.2 Greypillar will notify Customer of a Security Incident without undue delay, and no later than 48 hours after becoming aware of it. In DPA Standard Terms Section 11.15, "Security Incident" also includes a breach of personal data under the PDPA and any other Applicable Data Protection Laws.

8.3 The notice includes, as far as known at the time, and then as it becomes known: the nature of the incident, the categories and approximate numbers of data subjects and records concerned, the likely consequences, the measures taken or proposed, and a contact person. Greypillar will not notify a supervisory authority or data subject about Customer Personal Data on Customer's behalf without Customer's instruction, unless Applicable Laws require it to.

## 9 Audits and information {#audits status=planned}

> In plain words: Greypillar has no independent audit report. So you may ask it questions once a year, see its self-assessment, and audit it yourself when you need to, with notice.

9.1 This section replaces DPA Standard Terms Section 5.2 and changes the last two sentences of Section 5.1 and Section 5.3.

9.2 Greypillar is not audited by independent third parties and holds no Report. Greypillar holds no security certification, and Annex II says which measures are in place and which are not. Greypillar will give Customer, on request, its current self-assessment against the Cloud Security Alliance questionnaire (published in draft at /trust/star, labelled self-assessed), and its security overview at /security.

9.3 Greypillar will answer Customer's reasonable written questions about its compliance with this DPA, including security and due diligence questionnaires, once in each 12 months, and also after a Security Incident affecting Customer Personal Data or when a supervisory authority asks Customer.

9.4 Where those answers do not reasonably show compliance, or a supervisory authority requires it, or after a Security Incident affecting Customer Personal Data, Customer may audit Greypillar's compliance with this DPA, itself or through an independent auditor bound by confidentiality. Customer gives at least 30 days' notice, or less where a supervisory authority requires. Audits are done remotely by default, on site only where a remote audit cannot meet the purpose, during business hours, and without access to other customers' data. Each party bears its own costs, except that Customer pays Greypillar's reasonable costs for a second or later audit in the same 12 months that is not required by a supervisory authority or a Security Incident.

9.5 Greypillar keeps records of its Processing for Customer as Article 30(2) of the GDPR and section 40 of the PDPA require, and records of its compliance with this DPA for 3 years after the DPA ends.

## 10 Help with requests and assessments {#assistance status=planned}

> In plain words: If a person asks about their data, or you need to assess a risk, Greypillar helps without charge unless the help is far beyond the usual.

10.1 This section adds to DPA Standard Terms Section 6.

10.2 Greypillar provides the assistance in DPA Standard Terms Sections 6.1 and 6.2 without charge, except where a request is manifestly excessive, in which case it may charge its reasonable costs after telling Customer the amount in advance. The Service lets Customer find, export, correct and delete Customer Personal Data where its functions allow.

## 11 Return and deletion {#deletion status=planned}

> In plain words: When the agreement ends, you can take your data with you first. Then we delete it on the timetable in your agreement, and confirm it if you ask.

11.1 This section changes DPA Standard Terms Section 7.2.

11.2 After the Agreement ends, Customer may export Customer Personal Data during the export period the Agreement states. Greypillar then deletes it on the timetable in the Agreement (section 14 of the customer agreement, section 13 of the pilot agreement), unless further storage is required by Applicable Laws, and certifies deletion in writing if Customer asks.

## 12 Transfers {#transfers status=planned}

> In plain words: Your data is stored in the place your agreement names, but people and services outside it can reach it, and the law treats that as a transfer. For data leaving Europe, the UK or Switzerland, the official EU clauses apply, with the UK and Swiss changes. For data leaving Thailand, the same clauses are used as Thailand's law allows.

12.1 This section adds to DPA Standard Terms Section 3.

12.2 **Where data is.** Customer Personal Data is stored in the location stated in the Agreement. Unless the Order Form states another location, that is Helsinki, Finland, with the Subprocessor named for storage at /trust/subprocessors. It may be accessed from the places in section 7.2, and Processed by Subprocessors in the countries that the list at /trust/subprocessors or the Order Form states.

12.3 **EEA.** DPA Standard Terms Section 3.2 applies to transfers of Customer Personal Data protected by the GDPR, including access from outside the EEA to data stored in the EEA. Module Two applies where Customer is a Controller and Module Three where Customer is a Processor.[^2] In Clause 9(a) the period is 30 days (section 6.6). Clause 17 Option 1 and Clause 18 use the Governing Member State, Ireland. Annexes I, II and III of this DPA complete Annexes I, II and III of the EEA SCCs.

12.4 **United Kingdom.** DPA Standard Terms Section 3.3 applies. In Table 1 of the UK Addendum the parties' details are those in Annex I(A). In Table 2 the Addendum EU SCCs are the EEA SCCs as completed in section 12.3. In Table 3 the Appendix Information is in Annexes I, II and III. In Table 4, neither party may end the UK Addendum under its Section 19.[^3]

12.5 **Switzerland.** DPA Standard Terms Section 3.4 applies. Where Swiss law applies to a transfer, the Swiss Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner is a competent supervisory authority, the term "member state" in Clause 18(c) of the EEA SCCs does not exclude data subjects in Switzerland from bringing claims where they habitually reside, and the EEA SCCs protect personal data of legal entities only to the extent Swiss law requires.

12.6 **Thailand.** Where the PDPA applies to the transfer of Customer Personal Data out of Thailand, and no adequacy decision of the Personal Data Protection Committee ("PDPC") covers the destination, the parties rely on appropriate safeguards under PDPA section 29 in the form of standard contractual clauses. The parties adopt the EEA SCCs, Module Two or Three as in section 12.3, for that purpose, read with "GDPR" meaning the PDPA, "supervisory authority" meaning the PDPC, and "Member State" meaning Thailand where the clause concerns the rights of data subjects in Thailand. If the PDPC requires a different form of clauses, additional content, or a filing for such a transfer, the parties will sign that form, add that content or make that filing, and Greypillar will bear its own costs of doing so. Open: confirmation at source of the forms of clauses the PDPC's notification of 25 December 2023 recognises, and whether a filing is required.

12.7 **Other countries.** Where other Applicable Data Protection Laws require a transfer mechanism, the parties will agree the mechanism those laws accept, on terms no less protective than this DPA.

## 13 Liability {#liability status=planned}

> In plain words: The agreement's cap applies here too, with exceptions. For customers in the EU and the UK, liability for breaking the EU clauses or the UK Addendum has no cap: the standard terms say so in section 8.3. A person's own rights over their data are never limited. Greypillar handles no customer's personal data, in any country, until its insurance is in place.

13.1 DPA Standard Terms Section 8 applies. Nothing in the DPA or the Agreement excludes or limits liability for fraud, gross negligence or willful misconduct, or any liability that Applicable Laws do not allow to be excluded or limited.

13.2 Under DPA Standard Terms Section 8.3, the DPA does not limit liability to an individual about their data protection rights, or liability between the parties for breach of the EEA SCCs or the UK Addendum. The Agreement's General Cap Amount therefore does not apply to that liability.

13.3 Greypillar will not Process Customer Personal Data for any customer, in any country, until its insurance is in place.

## 14 Order of documents and disputes {#precedence status=planned}

> In plain words: For personal data this agreement comes before the main agreement, and the EU clauses come before both. Their choice of court is kept.

14.1 DPA Standard Terms Sections 9 and 10 apply. The dispute terms of the Agreement do not limit Clause 18 of the EEA SCCs, the UK Addendum, or a data subject's right to bring a claim where the law allows.
