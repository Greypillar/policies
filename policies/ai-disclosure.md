## 1 What this covers {#scope status=live}

> In plain words: this applies wherever an AI agent works or writes for Greypillar.

1.1 This disclosure covers every message, brief or other text that an AI agent prepares for Greypillar, and every conversation in which an agent speaks for Greypillar.

1.2 An agent, here, is software that uses an AI model to read, draft or act within limits Greypillar sets.

## 2 Agents say they are agents {#say-so status=planned}

> In plain words: you will always know when you are dealing with an agent, and who is responsible for it.

2.1 {claim:responsibility.agent_disclosure}

2.2 If you ask whether you are dealing with a person or an agent, the answer is true. An agent never denies being an agent.

2.3 An agent never uses a staff member's name, never presents itself as a person, and never writes in a way meant to make you think a person wrote it.

2.4 The disclosure comes at the latest with the first message or the first exchange. It is in the text itself, not only in a footer.

## 3 What agents will do {#today status=planned decides until=opening}

> In plain words: Start is not open yet, and no agent works for Greypillar on this site today. When it opens, a person reads and replies. We plan to add agents later and will say so first.

3.1 Start is not open yet, and no agent acts for Greypillar on this site today.

3.2 When Start opens, a person at Greypillar reads each request and replies. No agent acts for Greypillar on this site at opening. {claim:channel.reply_time}

3.3 We plan that agents will draft a first brief from a Start request, and that a person will check each brief before it reaches you. An agent would read only the one request it is preparing the brief for, and only to read it. It could not send anything to you, change your request, or act outside that task. We announce this here at least 30 days before it starts.

3.4 Agents do not answer email on their own.

## 4 Which models will do the work {#models status=planned}

> In plain words: no AI model sees your Start request yet. Before one does, we say here which providers, where, and what they keep.

4.1 No AI model processes what you send through Start yet. We plan that agents will reach models through OpenRouter, Inc. Before they do, we announce it at least 30 days ahead, and the [Subprocessors](/trust/subprocessors) list or the Order Form names the model providers and the countries they work in.

4.2 We plan to set Greypillar's OpenRouter account to route only to those named providers, and only to endpoints with zero data retention, which OpenRouter defines as no routine storage after processing[^2]. OpenRouter states one exception, content flagged as possible child sexual abuse material, which may be kept for review.

4.3 Providers that do not keep your request cannot train on it. OpenRouter itself does not store the text of requests. It keeps records such as token counts and timing[^3].

4.4 [Subprocessors](/trust/subprocessors) lists OpenRouter and what it would handle.

## 6 Limits, and how far to rely on a brief {#limits status=planned}

> In plain words: when agents write briefs, a brief is a first draft. It can be wrong. Tell us and a person corrects it.

6.1 When agents prepare briefs, a brief is provisional. AI models can misread a request, leave out what matters, or state something that is not true. The check by a person reduces this risk. It does not remove it.

6.2 A brief or a reply is not advice and creates no agreement. What Greypillar will do for you is set only by an agreement you accept.

6.3 If a brief or a reply is wrong, say so. A person corrects it.

## 7 The EU AI Act {#eu-ai-act status=live}

> In plain words: European law requires AI to be disclosed in some cases. This is how we meet it. We read the law in secondary sources and have not checked every provision on EUR-Lex.

7.1 Article 50 of the EU AI Act applies from 2 August 2026[^1]. We summarise it from secondary sources and have not checked every provision at EUR-Lex. It has three parts that could reach us. People must be told when they interact directly with an AI system, unless it is obvious. Providers of AI systems that generate text must mark the output in a machine-readable way (Article 50(2)). AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must be disclosed, unless a person has reviewed it and someone holds editorial responsibility.

7.2 The Digital Omnibus on AI, as the secondary sources describe it, gives providers of systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 until 2 December 2026 to meet Article 50(2). Greypillar runs no public service yet, so it has no such system on the market. If it provides one that generates text for others, it will mark the output as Article 50(2) requires, by the date that applies to it.

7.3 We meet the first rule through section 2. When agents begin to write to people, each message will also say in its text that agents prepared it and a person checked it. The line will be: "Prepared by Greypillar's agents and checked by a person before it reached you. Responsible: the accountable lead."

7.4 What Greypillar publishes on its website is reviewed by a person, and Greypillar holds editorial responsibility for it.
